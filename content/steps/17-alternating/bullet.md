**Q:** How do we build fronts with several crossings? At which thresholds can they exist?

- Here $L_1=0.8$, $L_2=1.6$, twice the earlier McKean lengths;
  $\alpha=1/4$ is unchanged.
- For ordered interfaces $\rho_1<\rho_2<\rho_3$,
  set $W=V_{\rho_1}-V_{\rho_2}+V_{\rho_3}$.
- front $\iff W(\rho_i)=a$; triangles mark matched positions only after a successful solve; drag a crossing (or keys 1 2 3, then , .) → red bar
- every front with $m$ crossings: alternating sum of $m$ profiles, $m$ odd
- Insets sample $W$ at $x=\rho_i+kL$, for $i=1,3$.
  Small black dots are samples; the large black ring is the interface sample.
  The large blue/orange dots are straight-branch endpoints on $u=a$.
- For a matched nonmonotone front, the endpoint ordering gives
  $b(\rho_1)<a<b(\rho_3)$, hence strict inclusion in the pinning interval.
  Unmatched samples need not form a return-map orbit.
- $P$ and $Q$ name the single-edge and stretched-pair crossing branches.
  For widely separated crossings, downward phases lie near roots
  of $b(s)=a$, and upward phases near roots of $b(s)=1-a$.
- Fix an interior $a$ and odd $m\ge3$. In $m$ prescribed, sufficiently
  separated periods there are exactly $2^m$ fronts with one crossing
  in each period and none elsewhere, one for each word.
- click a letter P or Q to change the word
- drag $a$: signs persist for matched nonmonotone fronts only; at this fixed separation words can be lost
